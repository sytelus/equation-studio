How to use Equation Studio
Every picture here is made from math. For each pixel, a small program works out its color, and your graphics chip runs it for every pixel, many times a second. You can open that math, change it, and watch the picture change.
1 · Look
Pick a scene with ☰ Scenes (top left). Press ▶ or Space to play it. Above the picture you choose what it shows: the Whole picture, Just this part, What this part adds, or What moves. A label on the picture always says which.
2 · Play
Anything orange can be changed: drag a slider, drag an orange number in code sideways, or drag an orange letter in the math. The panel’s Try this challenges change one thing each, and 🎲 Surprise me picks random settings. You cannot break anything: Undo (Ctrl/⌘ Z) takes back each change and Start over brings back the scene.
3 · Take it apart
The panel on the right tells the story of the scene: what you see, how it works, and the parts it is built from. The small pictures under the canvas are the same parts, in order. Click one to open it: its Math or Code, Look inside (every value the code computes, shown on the picture) and its Big ideas. ‹ at the top of the panel goes back to the whole scene.
4 · Read the colors
The same colors mean the same thing everywhere: orange is something you can change, pink is time (like the letter t and the time bar), blue is what comes into a part (like where the pixel is), and green is what a part makes.
5 · Make your own
＋ Add a part (under the picture) adds a building block. ✎ Edit opens a part’s math or code as text: the picture shows your edit as a DRAFT until you Apply it. In Wiring, drag between dots to connect parts. Save keeps your scene as a file; Export makes pictures, videos, GIFs or a web page.
6 · Go deeper
In a part, Connections shows what it uses and what uses it, Measure gives its numbers, a histogram and whether the loop is seamless, and More has its shader code and keyframes. The View menu has rulers, a grid, Measure along a line, brightness and sharpness. ◐ Contrast (footer, or K) switches to high contrast.
Is the picture live? Yes. Nothing is a saved image except the small pictures in the Scenes gallery: every pixel is computed from the math whenever anything changes. The LIVE badge blinks on each new frame and says whether your graphics chip (GPU) is doing the work. When you change how parts are connected, the chip needs a moment to get the new program ready; the last picture stays up meanwhile.
Keys: Space play or pause · Home/End start or end · , . one frame back or forward · [ ] previous or next part · I just this part · C what this part adds · M what moves · Esc close, or back to the whole picture · hold O to compare with the start · E wide panel · K contrast · V measure along a line · R rulers · G grid · F fit · P small pictures · S snapshot · L find in the wiring · Ctrl/⌘ Z undo · Ctrl/⌘ Shift Z redo · Ctrl/⌘ S save · Ctrl/⌘ D duplicate a part · Ctrl/⌘ Enter apply an edit · Delete delete a part (in the wiring)
On the picture: drag to move the view, use the wheel or pinch to zoom, click to pin a reading, Alt-click for the numbers at one spot. In math, drag an orange letter sideways (Shift for small steps); in code, drag an orange number, or click it and use the arrow keys. On a touch screen, press and hold a control to see what it does. A saved scene file keeps every part, setting, color, keyframe and the camera. Snapshots and pictures you compare with stay in this browser.
